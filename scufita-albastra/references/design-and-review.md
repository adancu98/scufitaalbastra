# Art direction and visual review

Use this reference for open design briefs, not for a single requested technical correction.

## Establish a visual direction

Translate the brief into observable goals: the focal element, visual hierarchy, atmosphere, viewing distance, reading order and what the design must communicate. “Premium” may mean restraint, expressive illustration, rich material or dramatic photography depending on the brief; it does not mandate a dark background, gold type, neon or a fixed grid.

For a loose brief, explore two or three different directions in words, a rough composition or generated concepts when generation is useful and authorized. Change the hierarchy or visual idea, not only the color. Choose against the intended audience, assets and constraints; do not force a user approval round unless it was requested or needed for a real missing requirement/tool boundary.

Inspect supplied references. Note useful properties (scale, rhythm, contrast, typography, light, material and negative space). Preserve originality. Without approved references, make a provisional direction and label it as your choice rather than the user's established taste.

## Plan before detailed execution

State the intended focal hierarchy and assign each essential element a role and space. A composition plan can be a short list rather than a rigid template. Keep editable type and original logos separate from generated imagery. Match perspective, light direction, horizon, contact shadows, grain and color when compositing. Do not solve every design as a set of unrelated rectangular photographs.

Image models can visualize the complete idea or provide individual assets. A whole-image concept is not an editable master. Preserve the successful relationships while reconstructing native text and geometry and using independent image assets in Photoshop. Decomposition is optional and does not recover original typography or source layers by itself.

## Review the actual artwork

Check the full composition at intended viewing scale and inspect detail crops at source-pixel resolution. Record concise observations and any changes made. Assess only what is visible:

| Criterion | Evidence to inspect |
| --- | --- |
| Brief fidelity | Exact copy, required products/content, format and constraints |
| Hierarchy and composition | Clear focal point, useful scale, coherent spacing, reading order and edge treatment |
| Integration | Perspective, lighting, contact, shadows, mask edges, color and texture |
| Product/content integrity | Authentic geometry, branding, labels, anatomy and factual details where applicable |
| Typography | Exact Romanian diacritics, glyphs, line breaks, spacing, legibility, contrast and clipping |
| Craft | Unintended halos, banding, cutoffs, muddy contrast, oversharpening and repetitive generated texture |
| Editability | Relevant text, masks, groups, Smart Objects and adjustments confirmed separately in the saved PSD |
| Delivery | Correct dimensions, profile, output format, actual pixel resources and saved-file readability |

Each relevant criterion is `pass`, `needs_revision` or `not_checked`, with evidence. A required product error, unreadable critical type or damaged master cannot be averaged away by an attractive composition. A numerical self-score is optional and is not user approval.

For complex or uncertain work, delegate an independent visual review with the user's brief, the current preview and relevant detail crops. Do not supply the intended grade or ask the reviewer to confirm success. The reviewer must inspect the images; filenames or a layer audit are insufficient aesthetic evidence. If delegation is unavailable, perform a separate review pass and state its scope.

Repair the largest visible weakness first. Inspect the changed result before another broad operation; avoid repeated global effects with no diagnosed purpose. If requirements remain unresolved, deliver the current draft as a draft with those limits.

## Retain useful feedback

Store explicit preferences and concrete accepted/rejected comparisons with project scope and provenance. Keep a small reusable set of approved references when the user supplies them. Preserve technical errors in the Adobe library's experience log when they concern a specific technique. Do not treat an automated critic's praise as a lasting aesthetic preference.
